# Story spine

The order a field-report page tells its story, with the job of each section and the mistake it prevents. Adjust the beats to the product; keep the order of ideas: orient, explain, let them push on it, show the platform, prove it, admit the gaps, then ask.

## The spine

1. **Hero: the object.** Breadcrumb top left, large display headline, a quiet link to the product top right. The hero object (default: an exploded stack of layers, animated in SVG) is the page, not an illustration beside copy. Bracketed numbered legend with arrow-lists of real components. Scroll cue bottom left.
   Job: say what this is and make it memorable in the first viewport.
2. **Problem and response.** Two short beats: what was broken or missing, and what was built. Plain operator language, not invented jargon.
   Job: a reader who skips everything else still gets the argument.
3. **The object turning.** The pinned scroll sequence: the layers orbit, one caption per layer, each anchored to the active layer.
   Job: the one authored moment; it also teaches the layer model that the rest of the page uses.
4. **Capabilities to push on.** Three or four interactive figures, one per real capability (for example network analysis, a what-if model, an agent trace, a governed action). Each draws from a baked snapshot.
   Job: show the product's gems. Do not skip this to make room for results.
5. **A map or spatial figure** if the product has a spatial story. Optional; include only with real data.
6. **The platform.** An interactive architecture with traced paths for a question and an action; mark the cloud services that carry each step. For a cloud-provider hackathon, name the services and put the one the judges care about first.
   Job: the proof that the system is built, not slides.
7. **Proof.** A results ledger with charts that draw only printed values; red lines or invariants as tested rules; before/after field notes.
   Job: measured, not claimed.
8. **Misses.** The ledger includes what failed or was not measured, in the warn colour.
   Job: a page with no gaps is not believed.
9. **Close.** One call to action, the credit or provenance line, and a push-in that ends on the product itself.

## Rules for the whole page

- Every section has a figure or a diagram; no long text-only stretches with an empty half-screen.
- One link into the product is always findable (nav, hero, close).
- Copy is plain and specific. A number appears once with its source and its caveat.
- The client or customer is named only if the user says so.

## A lesson from the first build

The first version leaned on measurement and under-communicated the problem, the solution and the capabilities. The user's note was that a case study should not only measure; it has to say what was wrong, what was built and what it can do, then prove it. Hence steps 2 and 4 above are not optional.
